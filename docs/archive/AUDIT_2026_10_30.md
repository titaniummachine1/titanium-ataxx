## 5. Zataxx (zzzzz151/Zataxx, Rust, 3 stars, no releases; local clone `C:\gitProjects\Zataxx` @ `b24e59f`)

**Net (read from `src/nn.rs`, our NNUE_EVAL_ANALYSIS PART 3b describes it wrongly):**
- Inputs: 36 overlapping **2x2 windows** (anchors (i,j), i,j in 0..6), each window = 4 cells x {empty, us, them}
  = 3^4 = 81 states -> 36 x 81 = **2,916 one-hot inputs, exactly 36 active per position**. Side-to-move relative.
  **No blocker/gap input.** No mobility, no landing, no exchange features — just local 2x2 shape.
- 2,916 -> 256 (i16, QA 255) -> SCReLU -> 1 (QB 64), eval x 400. Single perspective, no output buckets.
- Evaluated by **full refresh every node** (36 x 256 adds) — no accumulator; cheaper than our incremental W256 +
  head because there are only 36 active rows.
- Trained with bullet on its own self-play: 14-17 random opening plies, soft 5k / hard 10k nodes per move,
  openings with |score| > 300 dropped, adjudication at +-8000 (`src/datagen.rs`). Trainer config is NOT in the repo.

**Search (README + `src/search.rs`):** aspiration, PVS, TT, RFP, LMP, FP, multicut, IIR, LMR, singular +
negative extensions, ordering = TT move then most captures then history, soft/hard time + nodes TM,
tunable params. No NMP, no SMP.

**What it does better than us (hypotheses, games decide):**
1. Cheap eval -> many more nodes/s at fixed time (same story as Moonbird).
2. Ordering by capture count is free and strong in Ataxx; singular extensions + multicut on top.
3. Data/label recipe is simple and self-consistent (own search, 5-10k nodes, WDL from its own games).

## 5b. Zataxx's net, decoded and measured (numpy port of `nn.rs`, 120k random v7-val positions, bit-exact weights from `src/net6.bin`)

Method: exact re-implementation of `evaluate()` (36 overlapping 2x2 windows x 81 states, 2,916 -> 256 -> SCReLU -> 1, QA 255 / QB 64 / SCALE 400),
run on positions with OUR 5k-search label (`titanium_cp`) and the real game result. Script: scratchpad `zat_net.py` (one-off, not committed).

1. **A single static pass explains a lot of a 5,000-node search.** corr(Zataxx static eval, our 5k score) = **0.75 overall; 0.85 in the opening
   (4-16 stones), 0.90 in the middlegame (17-30), 0.69 late (31+)**, against 0.43 for plain stone difference (0.62 / 0.51 / 0.34 by phase). The tiny
   net is weakest exactly where forced tactics dominate (late game) - there search depth matters most, and that is where a cheap eval buys depth.
   Against the real game result: Zataxx static 0.31 vs our 5k search 0.36 (opening 0.11 vs 0.15, late 0.40 vs 0.50).
2. **It is NOT symmetric.** Evaluating the 7 other board symmetries (rotations/reflections) of the same position changes its eval by mean **81 cp** (p90 189,
   max 1,729; eval sd 521). So bullet's net was trained WITHOUT D4 augmentation (consistent with the repo, which shows no symmetrisation) and it still
   plays well; it also means free strength is left on the table (train with D4, or average 2 orientations at the root). Same lesson as our own D140.
3. **A stone's worth is mostly positional and large**: adding an own stone is worth +64..+100 cp in the interior, **+90..+105 on edges and +153..+156 in corners**;
   an enemy stone costs -24..-60 (interior/edge) and **-115..-122 in corners**. Corners are valued ~2x the centre in both directions; the centre is the cheapest
   square. Its cp are ~1.7x ours (slope of our 5k score per Zataxx cp = 0.58): scale conventions differ, ordering is what matters.
4. **Weights:** the heaviest feature rows are windows with several enemy stones (e.g. `TTU.`, `T.TT`, `.TUT`, norm ~640 vs 538 average); all-empty windows (13% of
   activations) are NOT zero (norm 591) - the net encodes "how much room is left" as well as who owns what. No blocker or mobility input exists; it infers
   mobility and capture threats only from local 2x2 shape.
5. **What it does not see** (and we do): reach/landing squares, capture counts, exposure (our G1-G6 groups) and a disagreement/uncertainty head.
   Its edge at equal TIME is therefore NPS (36 row-adds, no accumulator) - at equal NODES it LOSES (+81 Elo for Titanium, D166).
6. Gate logs of the 5k leg carry moves but no scores: `main`'s `serve` prints no `info ... score` line (it exists only on `exp/ti-hub`). Fix before the next gate
   so `ti analyze` / `ti harvest` can show what Titanium thought.

## 5c. What do the nets INFER? Shape probe, ours vs Zataxx vs our 5k search (docs/analysis/shape_probe.py, 38,757 v7-val positions)

Signals, all from the side to move: our 5k-node search score (reference), OUR net at depth 1 (`label --max-depth 1`: its judgement after one move),
ZATAXX net at depth 1 (`go depth 1`). Twelve hand-built geometric features, side-to-move relative: stone difference, corner / edge stones,
mobility (reachable targets), best capture available to the mover, best capture threat against the mover, FRONTIER stones (stones touching an empty
square = capturable), HOLES (empty squares with no empty neighbour; enemy-adjacent / own-adjacent), solid 2x2 blocks, empties left.

- **Agreement with the 5k search:** our net 0.753, Zataxx 0.635, stone difference alone 0.329. Opening / middle / late: ours 0.946 / 0.934 / 0.686,
  Zataxx 0.904 / 0.873 / 0.611. Both nets are weakest late (31+ stones), where forced tactics decide: that is where search depth, i.e. speed, pays.
- **Only 28% of the search score is linear geometry** (R^2 0.284); the depth-1 nets are at 0.46 / 0.45, so they use more than these 12 shapes
  (their own non-linear pattern combinations), but about half of what the search knows is in neither net's one-move view.
- **What each is built from** (cp per +1 sd of the feature; search | ours | Zataxx):
  stone difference +269 | +231 | +447; **frontier stones (own minus enemy) -179 | -147 | -330** (exposed stones are the biggest geometric liability);
  corners +79 | +67 | +105; edges +53 | +43 | +97; best capture available +44 | +43 | +71; holes +50 | +44 | +118;
  enemy-adjacent holes -33 | -24 | -62; mobility +28 | +22 | +16; solid 2x2 blocks ~0 for all three.
  => our net weights the shapes almost exactly like the search does (ratio ~0.85 everywhere); Zataxx over-weights material (x1.7) and frontier (x1.8) and
  under-weights mobility. It trades accuracy for a cheap, material-heavy eval and relies on search depth to correct it.
- **What each misses** (residual vs search, correlated with a shape): both under-value positions with many empties left (+0.13 ours, +0.18 Zataxx) -
  a phase/tempo effect; Zataxx additionally misses frontier (+0.13) and solid blocks (+0.10). Ours has no shape above |0.13|.
- Limits: correlational (not neuron-level), depth-1 not pure static for our net, hand features only. Next level = per-neuron probing and counterfactual
  stone moves on OUR net (plan arm PV2 / T11), then `ti probe` so it is a tested tool and not a scratch script.

### 5f. How compressible is our big net? (w256_downscaled_ep7.pt = main net 7AE0122F, 26,196 x 256 feature transformer; scratch scripts compress.py)

CORRECTION FIRST: a first run of this analysis printed "every channel has a twin (|cos| = 1.00)". That was MY BUG (diagonal filled with -1, then abs() made it 1.0).
Correct numbers:
- **No twin channels.** Max |cos| of a channel to any other channel (after removing the part all channels share): mean 0.14, median 0.12, **0% above 0.5**. Mean |cos| over all pairs 0.03.
  The W512 -> W256 Net2Net merge (`net2net_downscale.py`) worked only because that W512 was itself a duplicating Net2Net UPSCALE (twins by construction). A 256 -> 32/64 downscale has no
  twins to merge: function-preserving merging is impossible, any small net derived from this one needs distillation.
- **Not low-rank.** Energy of the feature transformer in the top r channel-space directions: r=8 13.5%, 16 19.1%, **32 27.9%, 64 42.0%**, 128 64.6%, 192 83.7%. A rank-32 factorisation keeps 28%.
- **Importance is only mildly concentrated** (|FT column| x |outgoing L1|): top 32 channels 22% (uniform 12.5%), top 64 37% (25%), top 128 62% (50%).
- **Feature groups** (rows of the FT; weight energy share / mean row norm): G1 12,838 feats **45.0%** / 0.227; G2 4,803 feats 13.7% / 0.213; G3 7,938 feats **34.7%** / 0.253;
  G5 441 feats 5.3% / **0.438 (heaviest per feature)**; G6 176 feats 1.4% / 0.344. (There is no G4.)
- Consistent with D109/D110: a W32 distilled net is nearly as good as W64 (the small net's quality is limited by what a tiny trunk can hold, not by its width choice).

### 5g. Downscale METHODS to try for the small / cheap net (user 2026-10-30: "imagine downscale methods, not just a new net; a new net loses Elo")
Rules that apply (all in the docs already): D130 no from-scratch capacity change (-36.6/-39 Elo when it was done); Mistake 38/D140 D4 augmentation; Mistake 39/BEHAVIOR 31 val-MSE parity first,
never fine-tune a downscale at high LR; D162 exact mates; every candidate gated per node AND per second (D167).
The user quoted "-79 Elo" for a new net: NOT found in DECISIONS/LEDGER/HANDOFFS/MISTAKES/git messages of any worktree or in the CCD session transcripts (search done 2026-10-30);
documented from-scratch/new-net losses are -36.6..-39 (D130), -43 (D140), -137 (D98). The opencode history DB is searched separately (see HANDOFFS).
| id | method | what it keeps from the big net | risk / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **importance pruning + least-squares re-fit**: keep the top-r channels (r = 32/64) by importance, then re-solve L1 on activations of ~100k positions so the pruned hidden vector reproduces the original L1 pre-activations | weights of the best channels, exact for them | only 22-37% of importance is in 32-64 channels: expect big step-0 loss, measure val MSE |
| S2 | **channel clustering / merge**: k-means on ACTIVATION vectors (not weights), merged FT = mean of members, merged L1 = sum of members (the generalised twin merge) | all channels, approximately | no twins (cos < 0.5): approximation error is large, needs distillation after |
| S3 | **low-rank FT**: FT ~ A (26196 x r) . B (r x 256) | the whole input map | rank 32 keeps 28% energy; shrinks the accumulator, NOT the feature extraction |
| S4 | **feature-group restriction**: keep only cheap-to-extract groups (G1 square state is 45% of the energy), retrain trunk by distillation from the big net's outputs | the cheap half of the knowledge | the part that may decide NPS (extraction cost): profile first |
| S5 | **output distillation with two duties on one trunk**: an EVAL head (target = big-net / search score, D4, exact mates, WDL blend) and a BOUND head (pinball tau 2% on child evals, inputs parent static + hand eval as today). Trunk initialised from S1/S2/S4, distilled at LR <= 1e-5 (Mistake 39) | duties: cheap eval for most nodes + futility bound | replaces fut_small.tsn (stale, D168) and is the cheap net of BACKLOG row 0 |
| S6 | **Zataxx-style window net** (36 overlapping 2x2 windows x 81 states = 2,916 inputs, 36 active, 256 hidden) distilled from the big net | new representation, taught by the big net's outputs | not a downscale of weights; 0.66 static correlation with our search at ~20x speed (5d); needs D4 (their net is asymmetric, 5b) |

### 5h. Where our eval time goes (titanium-cli nnbench, 20,000 v7-val boards, idle box; docs/analysis/nnbench_big_net_2026_10_30.out)
Per EVALUATION of the default big net (7AE0122F, W=256, 26,196 features, mean 106.4 active per perspective, both perspectives):
- feature extraction, all groups: **1.41 us** (G1 square state 0.88, G2 anchor 0.71, G3 stone ring 0.81, G5 2-ply exchange 1.20, G6 global 0.69 - alone each; they share work)

- Compare Zataxx: 36 active rows x 256, one perspective, no feature extraction beyond two shifts: ~0.3 us/eval and ~0.6 us for the WHOLE node (1.4-1.8 Mnps).
- Our whole node costs 10-14 us (69-103 knps, D167): the eval is at most about half of it. The rest (movegen, TT, search bookkeeping, L1/L2 forward, the small-net futility path) is NOT yet profiled at instruction level.
CONCLUSION (measured, not yet gated): (1) a pure downscale of this net can cut the eval from 5.8 to ~2 us = at most ~1.4-2x NPS, not 17-20x; (2) closing the gap needs BOTH cheaper inputs (window features with ~36 active rows, or incrementally updated features) and a lighter search path; (3) the cheap-net/hybrid plan (BACKLOG 0) must be judged against a ~0.6 us/node budget, and the search-side overhead needs a real profile next.

| G5 2-ply exchange | 441 | 10.3 | 21 -> 429 | **~1,230 ns = 49% of all extraction** (alone: 2,146 ns, the most expensive group) | -0.041 (0.708 / **0.815** / 0.880 / 0.664): matters in the OPENING |
| G6 global | 176 | 3.0 | 6 -> 125 | ~13 ns | **-0.002** (0.746 / 0.935 / 0.920 / 0.679): worth almost nothing |
Combinations: drop G5+G6 -> 0.697 (-0.052, still above Zataxx's 0.664), extraction 2,521 -> 1,293 ns (-49%), rows 213 -> 187; drop G2+G5+G6 -> 0.686 (-0.062), rows 160; G1 alone 0.573.
(The standalone nnbench measures ALL groups at 1.41 us extraction; this harness shows 2.52 us because it returns two 696-byte arrays per call: use the marginals and ratios, not the absolute extraction numbers, and `nnbench` for absolute ones. Both runs agree that G5 is the extraction hog.)
FINDINGS: (1) **G1 + G3 are 76% of the active rows and carry almost all the value**; they are LOCAL features (a move touches <= 9 squares) => the standard fix is an INCREMENTAL accumulator for them (about 20-30 row updates per move instead of 160 refreshed rows): refresh 4.4 us could fall to roughly 1.5-2 us without touching the net. (2) **G6 can be dropped for free** (-0.002 corr, 6 rows). (3) **G5 is the best trade to attack**: half of all extraction time and 10 of 107 rows for -0.041 untrained (a W512 trained without G5 lost only 0.6% val loss earlier, D-notes), but it matters in the opening (open corr 0.939 -> 0.815): retrain without it and gate, or make its extraction cheaper. (4) Zataxx's inputs are the opposite design: 36 rows, no extraction, one perspective. NEXT RUNGS: input-trimmed nets (no G6; no G5+G6) = fine-tune from main with `train_nnue.py --drop`, then `ti gate --net-test --kind smaller`; and the incremental-accumulator engineering item (BACKLOG 0d).
### 5k. What are we MISSING in our inputs? (powered test, all 677,670 v7-val rows, 460k train / 200k test; docs/analysis/missing_inputs.py)
Question: does any input family explain the part of the 5k search score that our MAIN net (7AE0122F) does not? Our net: corr 0.822 with the search on 660,698 non-mate rows (the full val set is easier than the 40k probe sample: 0.749). Residual sd: opening 87 cp, middlegame 104, **late game 280** (corr 0.903 / 0.938 / **0.732**).
- **Stacking with Zataxx's net: held-out R^2 0.8831 -> 0.8835 (+0.0004).** Zataxx's static eval adds NOTHING our net does not already know (Zataxx alone: 0.7606).
- **Zataxx's 2x2-window family -> residual: held-out R^2 -0.0019** (train +0.005): nothing missing. The control (our own square-state input) reads -0.008 = the noise level of this test. (The first run on 19k rows was underpowered and gave a meaningless -0.05; capture maps +-0.01 and global counts +0.008 there are inside that noise.)
CONCLUSION (measured): Zataxx's inputs carry a SUBSET of the information in ours; its whole advantage is cost per node (D167), not knowledge. What our static eval misses is concentrated in the LATE game (residual 3x larger) = lookahead / forced tactics, which no static input family explains linearly. => (1) do NOT add new input families hoping for Elo; (2) the Elo levers are DATA/LABEL quality (esp. late-game labels: depth, mates, honest outcomes) and SPEED (trimmed inputs, incremental accumulators, smaller nets); (3) a window-input net is attractive only as a cheap eval, not as a source of new knowledge. Limits: linear residual probes cannot rule out non-linear interactions (G5, the 2-ply exchange group, is such an interaction and matters in the opening, 5j).
